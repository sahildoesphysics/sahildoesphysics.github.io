Sahil ShahCondensed matter physics

Hi, I’m Sahil.

I’m a physicist interested in condensed matter theory, especially topological order: how it shows up in quantum Hall fluids and at quantum critical points, and how experiments can detect it.

Since August 2025 I’ve been working with Prashant Kumar at IIT Bombay on quantum critical points in anyon gases, using large-N Chern–Simons–matter theories.

I came to theory by way of the cleanroom. For my master’s thesis at Institut Néel in Grenoble I built quantum Hall Josephson junctions in graphene nanoribbons with Benjamin Sacépé, and before that I spent a summer in Würzburg making Josephson junctions on magnetic topological insulators. Some of those devices are in the gallery, and there’s a quantum Hall bar to play with on the demos page.

I did an integrated BS-MS in physics at IISER Mohali, where I was also a teaching assistant for thermodynamics and statistical mechanics and ran phi@i, the physics club. I’m now applying to PhD programs in condensed matter physics for Fall 2027.
