The quantum Hall effect, from plateaus to fractions

A two-dimensional electron gas in a strong magnetic field splits into Landau levels. Disorder broadens each level: states in its tails are trapped on closed equipotentials and are localized, and only a narrow band near its centre is extended across the sample. Change the field and follow the Fermi level through the density of states, the electron gas and the measured resistance at the same time.

Switch on interactions and the electrons in a partly filled level bind flux quanta into composite fermions, which open the fractional plateaus. At the highest fields the last plateau gives way to a Hall insulator.

Off: independent electrons, up to 14 T. On: composite fermions and fractional plateaus, up to 24 T.

About the model

The sample is a GaAs 2DEG (m* = 0.067 me) with a fixed density of 1.0 × 1011 cm−2, so ν = 1 falls at 4.1 T and ν = 1/3 at 12.4 T. Levels are taken as spin-resolved, En = (n + ½)ħωc, each broadened by a smooth random potential with rms amplitude Γ. The Fermi level is found by filling states until the density is reached.

In a strong field the guiding centre of a cyclotron orbit drifts along equipotentials of the disorder, so a state of energy E in level n lives on the contour V(r) = E − En. The Hall bar draws those contours at the Fermi energy with marching squares, and the dots move with the E × B drift. Small closed contours are localized. Contours that link the two edges, or that are larger than an assumed phase-coherence length of 0.7 bar widths, count as extended and let electrons backscatter. The confining potential at the edges carries one chiral channel per filled level. The shading shows the local filling factor.

The range of energies with extended states is measured on this simulated sample, and the same range sets where σxy steps by e²/h. Each step follows the semicircle law σxx² + (σxy − N − ½)² = ¼. Past the last plateau σxy and σxx both vanish on the semicircle, so ρxx diverges while ρxy stays at h/e²: the quantized Hall insulator. When levels overlap (ħωc ≲ 3Γ) the transport crosses over to the Drude form with Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations.

With interactions on, the electrons in the partly filled level N (filling f) are treated in mean-field composite-fermion theory. Each binds two flux quanta, so they see B* = B(1 − 2f) and fill |ν*| = f/|1 − 2f| Λ levels. Those levels are spaced by a Coulomb gap Δ = 0.33 (e²/εℓB)|1 − 2f|, reduced by 1/(1 + N²) in higher levels, and are broadened by a quarter of the electron disorder. The composite fermions go through the same percolation and semicircle machinery. The electron resistivity is the composite-fermion one plus the Chern–Simons term, ρxy = ρ*xy + 2h/e², which turns ν* = ±p into the plateaus ν = p/(2p ± 1). The dashed contours are the composite-fermion states at their Fermi energy. For f > ½, B* is reversed and so is their drift. Near f = ½ the Λ levels merge into a composite-fermion Fermi sea. Past 1/3, the composite fermions localize and the sample again becomes a Hall insulator with ρxy ≈ 3h/e².

It is a teaching model. It captures the percolation and composite-fermion pictures, but not critical exponents, spin, or fractions outside the Jain sequences, such as 5/2.
