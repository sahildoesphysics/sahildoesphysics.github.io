Research

I’m broadly interested in condensed matter theory.

Current interests

  • Topological order and the fractional quantum Hall effect. Anyons, field-theory descriptions of fractional quantum Hall states, and the dualities that let different-looking theories describe the same physics.
  • Quantum critical points between topological phases. Transitions from quantum Hall states to insulators, and the conformal field theories that describe them.
  • Detecting topological order. All the ways experiments can see it, from transport to mesoscopic probes such as interferometry and noise measurements that get at the fractional statistics of anyons.
  • Superconductivity meets topology. Superconductors coupled to quantum Hall edges, and topological and nonequilibrium superconductivity, especially in graphene and moiré materials, where these ingredients can be tuned with a gate.

I’m interested in probing these questions with quantum field theory techniques, and I’m eager to learn numerics.

Projects

Anyonic quantum critical points

With Prashant Kumar, IIT Bombay · 2025 to now theory

Quantum critical points of anyon gases, such as the transition between a fractional quantum Hall state and an insulator, studied with large-N Chern–Simons–matter theories. A paper with Michael Mulligan and Prashant Kumar is in preparation.

A tunable quantum Hall Josephson junction in graphene

Institut Néel, CNRS Grenoble · MS thesis with Benjamin Sacépé · 2024–25 experiment

In a quantum Hall Josephson junction the supercurrent is carried by chiral edge states, here at ν = 1 and 2. I fabricated ultra-narrow junctions on encapsulated graphene nanoribbons with split gates inside them, which tune the junction’s Andreev bound-state spectrum and so make a tunable topological Josephson junction.

Thesis materials

Josephson junctions on magnetic topological insulators

Universität Würzburg · Laurens Molenkamp’s group, supervised by Charles Gould · 2023 experiment

I fabricated gate-tunable 2D topological Josephson junctions (SNS) and SNN devices on magnetically doped (Hg,Mn)Te/(Hg,Cd)Te quantum wells, then measured them at 1.5 K: Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations gave the carrier density and gating efficiency, and a Landau fan chart gave the gap voltage.

Electronics for qubit control

IIT Bombay · with Suddhasatta Mahapatra · 2022 instrumentation

Python drivers in QCoDeS for an RF instrument stack that delivers pulses for single-qubit control, and QCoDeS-controlled Hall-bar measurements.
